## Short answer
Turmeric or curcumin supplements **may modestly help pain and function in knee osteoarthritis**, but I would not rely on them as a main arthritis treatment. A pharmacist can check this for you for free, at any community pharmacy or through an NHS medicines review, because turmeric can interact with medicines.

## What the good evidence shows
Most evidence is for **osteoarthritis**, the “wear and repair” type of arthritis, especially knee osteoarthritis. Reviews of randomised trials found turmeric or curcumin was linked with reduced pain and better function compared with placebo, and some trials found similar symptom scores to anti-inflammatory painkillers. But one review also said the best dose, product type, and frequency remain unclear. ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33500785/))

For arthritis in general, a review covering several types, including rheumatoid arthritis, found possible improvements in pain and inflammation. But it also said the trials were small or low quality, so the conclusions need careful interpretation. ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35935936/))

## What is still uncertain
**The evidence here is unclear** for whether turmeric helps rheumatoid arthritis in a way that prevents joint damage. If you have rheumatoid arthritis, NHS advice says medicines such as DMARDs are used to slow the disease and reduce damage, so turmeric should not be treated as a replacement. ([nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/rheumatoid-arthritis/treatment/))

## Risks, side effects and interactions
Herbal medicines are not tested in the same way as standard medicines, and the NHS says they may not be suitable if you take medicines from a pharmacy or doctor. The NHS specifically lists possible problems with statins, antidepressants, diabetes medicines such as metformin, blood pressure medicines such as ramipril, and blood thinners such as warfarin. ([nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/herbal-medicines-and-complementary-therapies/))

If you take a blood thinner, speak to your GP, anticoagulant clinic or pharmacist before taking any supplement or herbal remedy. A review found turmeric was associated with bleeding risk in people on anticoagulants, which means medicines that reduce blood clotting. ([nhs.uk](https://www.nhs.uk/medicines/anticoagulants/considerations/))

## Questions to ask your GP or pharmacist
1. “Could turmeric interact with my full medicines list, including blood thinners, diabetes, blood pressure, heart, pain or cholesterol medicines?”
2. “Do I have osteoarthritis or rheumatoid/inflammatory arthritis, and does that change the advice?”
3. “What should I watch for if I try it, and when should I stop and seek advice?”
