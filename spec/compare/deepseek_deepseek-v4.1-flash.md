# deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash

## Short answer
Pooled analyses of trials suggest turmeric extracts ease knee osteoarthritis pain more than a dummy tablet (placebo), and about as much as anti-inflammatory painkillers. But the evidence is short-term, mostly about knee osteoarthritis, and rated low to moderate quality. Turmeric is a supplement, so a pharmacist can check it against your medicines for free — that check matters here.

## What the good evidence shows
- Systematic reviews (studies that pool the results of many randomised controlled trials — trials where people are randomly given either the treatment or a placebo) found turmeric extracts reduced knee osteoarthritis pain and improved physical function compared with placebo. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33511486/)
- A review of 16 trials, about 1,810 adults, found turmeric worked about as well as non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs — common anti-inflammatory painkillers) and had 12% fewer unwanted effects than NSAIDs, with rates similar to placebo. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33511486/)
- Another pooled analysis found no significant difference in pain scores between turmeric or curcumin and painkillers. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5003001/)
- People with a higher body mass index (BMI — weight relative to height) got less benefit. [pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33511486/)

## What is still uncertain
The evidence here is unclear in important ways. Reviews rate the certainty low to very low, trials lasted up to 16 weeks, and results varied a lot between studies — so the true size of any benefit is uncertain. A recent umbrella review said it is not yet possible to say which curcumin formulation works best. Reviews found no significant change in blood markers or scans, so this is about symptom relief, not joint repair. Most evidence is knee osteoarthritis. For rheumatoid arthritis, gout and other types, one review found too few good trials to conclude. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9353077/)

## Risks, side effects and interactions
In these trials, unwanted effects were similar to placebo and fewer than with NSAIDs. However, I found nothing in the pages I retrieved about interactions with prescription medicines, so I cannot tell you whether turmeric is safe alongside your current medicines. Supplements are not held to the same standards as prescription medicines. Do not stop or change prescribed arthritis medicine yourself.

## Questions to ask your GP or pharmacist
1. Is my arthritis osteoarthritis or an inflammatory type — does this evidence even apply to me?
2. Can you check turmeric against all my medicines, including blood pressure, blood thinners, diabetes, heart and pain relief?
3. If I try it, what should I watch out for, and when should I come back?
